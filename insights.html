Frequently Asked Investor Questions

Direct, transparent answers regarding our fiduciary standards, fee models, advisory scope, and portfolio oversight.

A fiduciary advisor is legally and ethically bound to act solely in the best interests of the client at all times. Traditional brokers, in contrast, frequently operate under a lower "suitability standard," permitting them to recommend products that generate higher commissions or sales incentives for their firm, provided the product is considered nominally suitable. Wealth Bridge operates exclusively under a fiduciary mandate with zero proprietary product quotas.

We adhere to transparent, tiered fee structures based on assets under advisory (AUA) or flat retainer fees for complex wealth planning mandates. We do not charge transaction commissions, performance carry fees that encourage reckless risk-taking, or accept soft-dollar kickbacks from third-party fund managers. All fees are clearly disclosed in advance in your advisory agreement.

No. Reputable, fiduciary investment advisors never guarantee returns. Capital markets fluctuate, and past performance is never an assurance of future results. Our role is to optimize factors within your control—asset allocation, cost reduction, tax location, and disciplined risk mitigation—to maximize the probability of achieving your life goals while prudently managing downside volatility.

Wealth Bridge never takes direct physical custody of client funds or securities. All client assets are held in independent, SIPC-insured institutional custodians (such as Charles Schwab, Fidelity Institutional, or Pershing) registered in the client's own name. Clients retain sole title and complete online access to their independent custodian accounts at all times.

Portfolios are subjected to continuous algorithmic drift monitoring and formal quarterly committee reviews. Rather than rebalancing on arbitrary calendar dates (which can generate unnecessary transaction costs and taxable events), we employ systematic "threshold rebalancing"—realigning assets only when an asset class crosses designated percentage bands relative to its strategic target.
Investment Strategy

Asset Allocation Across Shifting Economic Cycles

Author: Jonathan Vance, CFA | Reading Time: 7 Minutes

Traditional 60/40 balanced portfolios rely heavily on a negative correlation between equities and government bonds. However, during periods characterized by supply-side inflation and tightening monetary regimes, equities and nominal bonds frequently decline in tandem.

Our research demonstrates that incorporating real assets, inflation-protected sovereign debt (TIPS), and global non-dollar equity exposure creates an all-weather foundation that preserves purchasing power across shifting macroeconomic regimes.

Key Takeaway: Fiduciary asset allocation must account for multi-decade inflation regimes rather than assuming low-volatility conditions will persist indefinitely.

Wealth Planning

The Psychology of Long-Term Investing Discipline

Author: Eleanor Chen, CFP® | Reading Time: 5 Minutes

Empirical behavioral finance studies show that the average equity investor underperforms broad market indexes by 200–300 basis points annually due to emotional trading—selling during steep corrections and buying during speculative bull runs.

We examine the role of a formal Investment Policy Statement (IPS) in setting rules-based drawdown guardrails and maintaining behavioral fortitude when headlines induce anxiety.

Key Takeaway: Successful investing is often more a test of temperament and psychological discipline than raw intellectual calculation.

Risk Management

Tax-Aware Portfolio Rebalancing: Preserving Net Returns

Author: Marcus Holloway, ChFC® | Reading Time: 8 Minutes

Gross portfolio returns mean little if excessive taxes and turnover costs diminish net family wealth. This paper explores the quantitative principles of asset location: holding tax-inefficient assets (such as high-yield debt and REITs) in tax-deferred vehicles, and high-growth equities in taxable accounts.

Furthermore, systematic tax-loss harvesting during temporary market pullbacks generates valuable tax offsets that compound significantly over 10- and 20-year horizons.

Key Takeaway: What you keep after taxes and advisory fees is the only return that ultimately funds your milestones.
